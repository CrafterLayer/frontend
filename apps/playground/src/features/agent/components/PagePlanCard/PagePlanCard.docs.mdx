# PagePlanCard

The page plan, drawn in pencil (SPEC §3.7, §4.11): Plan mode's answer to a page request, and Ask
mode's approval for one. It is a decision surface, so it has a border; everything that already
happened in the thread has none.

<Canvas of={PagePlanCardStories.Ready} />

## Purpose

A page request is expensive and hard to take back. The card shows the plan the engine will build
from (the subject, the look and the sections in page order) and lets the person change it before
anything touches the page. **"Build this page" is the approval.** When it is pressed, the card's box
dissolves and the same list inks into the build in place (SPEC §5 moment 4): the plan the person
approved is visibly the plan being built.

## When to use / not

Use it for a `page-plan` turn: Plan mode on a page request, and Ask mode's page approval (with the
"Waiting for your approval" tag). While the planner writes, it is a working record, not a card.

Do not use it for an edit plan (StepPlanCard) or an Ask edit (ProposalCard). Do not show it after
Build: the turn is then a BuildRecord under the same turn id.

## Anatomy

e1: the Sheet, a 1px Hairline, radius 9, 12px in. Full-width Hairlines with 8px above and below
split it into four parts:

1. **Head**: the title (Heading, 600, Ink), a Tint tag at the inline end ("Edited", or "Waiting for
   your approval" in Ask mode), and the subject in Graphite. No node: the plan's nodes are its
   sections'.
2. **DirectionEditor**: the mood as a field that reads as its line, five swatches, the font pair.
   Without structured direction it is a read-only DirectionLine of the direction prose.
3. **PlanSectionList** on the pencil spine, then **AddSectionRow**. Node, number and title sit
   exactly where the build's spine will draw them.
4. **Footer**: "Build this page" (the Indigo fill), "Discard plan" (Indigo words), and one Meta note
   under them: "Builds exactly the sections shown." or "The built page may differ from this
   outline." When Build waits, the reasons replace the note, one Amber line each.

## Accessibility

- An `<article>` named by its `h3` ("Page plan"); the heading takes `tabindex="-1"`.
- The list is an `<ol>` named "Sections (8)". Every row control names its section ("Reorder
  Doctors", "Options for Doctors"); the handle is described by the drag hint.
- Reorder three ways, as WCAG 2.5.7 asks: drag the handle, Alt+↑ / Alt+↓, ⋯ → Move up / Move down.
  Each move is announced through LiveStatus (`plan.moved`), the panel's one live region; the card
  carries none of its own.
- Build waits `aria-disabled`, never `disabled`: it stays in the tab order, described by the reason
  written under it. Remove at two sections and Add at twelve do the same.
- Focus names its destination (SPEC §6.3), asserted by name in the tests: after Build, the new build
  record's head; after Discard, the composer; after a move, the moved row's control; after a
  removal, the `[⋯]` of the row that took its place.

## RTL

Logical properties only. In Arabic the pencil runs down the right edge and the handle and `[⋯]` sit
at the left; the tag sits at the head's inline end. Titles and the subject take their own direction
from their script (`resolveDir`), inside `<bdi>`.

## Motion

- Rows arrive in page order, 24ms apart, opacity and 4px (SPEC §5 moment 6).
- Build: the controller swaps the turn inside a view transition. Each row is named
  `ck-plan-row-{key}` and the head `ck-plan-head`; the rows hold still and glide the 12px the
  padding gives back (`enter`, 220ms), while the box fades (`exit`, 140ms). For the rows to morph
  rather than cross-fade, the BuildRecord's head and spine rows must carry the same names.
- Reduced motion: no arrival, no view transition; the swap is instant.
