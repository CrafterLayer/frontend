# AssistantComposer

The bottom of the assistant panel: the field, the mode row, the send slot, and the two lines that
say what the next press will do.

<Canvas of={AssistantComposerStories.Empty} />

## One control, not three

The focus ring is drawn around the whole card by `:focus-within`, and the textarea delegates its own
with `data-bldr-focus-ring="delegated"`. Tabbing from the field to Send therefore does not slide a
ring off the thing the person is looking at — the card stays lit for as long as any part of it holds
focus.

`Enter` sends and `Shift+Enter` writes a line: the convention every chat uses, and the one a
person's hands already know.

<Canvas of={AssistantComposerStories.Written} />

## `@` reads what is BEFORE the caret

<Canvas of={AssistantComposerStories.MentionOpen} />

The mention in progress is matched against `value.slice(0, caret)`, not the whole field. Someone
editing the middle of a sentence that happens to end in `@hero` is not writing that mention, and
opening a picker there would put a list under a caret that is somewhere else entirely. `Escape`
closes the picker without clearing the text.

## Busy: the modes lock, and the line says why

<Canvas of={AssistantComposerStories.Busy} />

A run captures its mode when it starts and the closure keeps it. A request begun in Auto carries
`apply: 'auto'` all the way to `applyAiPlan`. Leaving the mode control enabled meant a person could
press Auto, see it thinking, switch to Plan — and watch the page be written anyway, by a run they
had just told to stop writing. Locking is the honest answer, because it is true: this run's
authority was decided when it started, and the way to change it is Stop.

The explanation lives on the footnote line, not among the buttons. The first version put it inside
the radiogroup, where a three-line paragraph wedged between the modes and Send wrapped over the
action row and made the composer look broken. A one-line explanation belongs in the one-line place a
person already reads for mode information.

## One slot, two verbs

While the assistant works, the send slot IS the stop control — the same place the hand just was, and
a promise that a running request is never out of reach. Its accessible name changes with it, from
`Send` to `Stop`.

## Refined presentation

The idle composer uses a quiet theme border; the focus ring and busy feedback remain visible. Its
short undo assurance avoids a multi-line footer.
