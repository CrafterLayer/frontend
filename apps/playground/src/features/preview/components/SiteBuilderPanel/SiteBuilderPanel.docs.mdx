# SiteBuilderPanel

One sentence in, a whole site out — on its own destination on the rail.

<Canvas of={SiteBuilderPanelStories.Named} />

## Why it is not a card in the assistant

It was, for one afternoon. The first person to use it said the conversation and their own typing had
become hard to tell apart, and they were right: a three-line form, a hint, a status line and an
outcome sat permanently between the header and the messages, and the messages got whatever was left
of the panel.

The deeper reason is that **a site build is not a message**. A conversation is a sequence of turns
someone reads back; a build is a job with five stages, its own progress and its own Stop, and it
stays interesting for minutes after the sentence that started it. Putting a job in a message list
makes the job hard to watch and the list hard to read.

## One lease, two destinations

The assistant panel owns the run lease and hands it here. That sharing is deliberate and it is the
only thing the two destinations have in common: there is one `activeRunRef`, one `busy` and one
`stop`, so a site build and a page request can never both believe they own the editor — whichever
destination a person happens to be looking at.

<Canvas of={SiteBuilderPanelStories.AnotherRunOwnsTheEditor} />

When another run holds the lease the form is **closed, not hidden**. A control that vanishes leaves
a person wondering what they did; a closed one says the door is locked and will open.

## When there is no site

<Canvas of={SiteBuilderPanelStories.NoSite} />

A blank panel and a panel with nothing to do look identical, and only one of them is a bug. This
says which it is, in words.
