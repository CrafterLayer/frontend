# SuggestionRail

What to say next, one tap away. It renders a list somebody else derived — see
`AssistantPanel.suggestions.ts` — and owns only how that list is shown and scrolled.

<Canvas of={SuggestionRailStories.Rail} />

## Two layouts, one list

On an empty panel there is nothing to crowd and a person who has said nothing yet is choosing, so
the suggestions stack as full-width targets. The moment a conversation exists they become a one-row
rail at a fixed height.

That split exists because the first version did not have it: the strip inherited the empty state's
styling, four full-width cards became permanent, and on a 360px panel they ate half the height and
hid the very thread they were meant to help continue. The owner caught it from a screenshot.

<Canvas of={SuggestionRailStories.Stack} />

## While the assistant works

The rail stays mounted and disables its chips. Unmounting it did two things a person feels rather
than reads: the chip they had just pressed vanished from under their finger, so keyboard focus fell
to `<body>` and the next Tab started over; and the composer lurched by the strip's whole height,
then lurched back when the answer arrived.

## Scrolling, and the RTL trap

Touch and trackpads scroll the rail natively. A mouse wheel only scrolls vertically, so the handler
translates the dominant axis — otherwise every chip past the edge is unreachable without dragging.

The sign matters more than it looks. `scrollLeft` follows the writing direction: in an Arabic panel
it starts at 0 on the RIGHT and runs negative leftward, so adding a positive delta scrolls back
toward the start you are already at. The rail simply refused to move, in the language this product
defaults to.

## Language, per chip

A rail can hold an Arabic example beside an English follow-up, so `lang` is set from each chip's own
text rather than from the panel's locale. Announcing one in the other's voice is exactly what that
attribute exists to prevent.

The chip truncates to one line; the whole sentence stays the accessible name, and `title` gives a
sighted reader the rest on hover. What is sent when it is pressed is the full sentence, unchanged.

## Refined presentation

Conversation suggestions snap one item at a time within a constrained scroll viewport. Previous and
Next buttons provide mouse and keyboard navigation; native horizontal touch and trackpad scrolling
remains available. Vertical wheel events are no longer converted into duplicate horizontal
scrolling. Short docks constrain the empty-state suggestion stack.
