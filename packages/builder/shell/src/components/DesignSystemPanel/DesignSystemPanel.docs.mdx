# DesignSystemPanel

A site's design system, edited one token at a time.

<Canvas of={DesignSystemPanelStories.Default} />

## One edit is one dispatch

The field holds a **draft** while a person types. A controlled input that dispatched on every
keystroke would put an entry in history per character, and "undo the colour" would become seven
presses instead of one.

<Canvas of={DesignSystemPanelStories.Editing} />

The commit happens on blur and on Enter. A value equal to the one already committed dispatches
nothing at all — "it did nothing" and "it did nothing but I recorded it anyway" are different lies
to tell a person reading their own history.

## An external write always wins

<Canvas of={DesignSystemPanelStories.ExternalWriteWins} />

If the system changes underneath — a generation lands, another tab commits, an undo runs — the draft
is **discarded** and the field shows what the site actually holds.

That direction is the uncomfortable one and it is deliberate. Keeping the draft would let a
half-typed colour outrank a value the kernel has already committed, and the next commit would
silently overwrite a change the person never saw. Losing three keystrokes is the smaller harm than
showing a site a colour it does not have.

## Refusals are announced, not drawn

<Canvas of={DesignSystemPanelStories.ContrastWarning} />

The **door** owns legibility: `setDesignToken` refuses a value that would push a pairing the site
declared as legible under its threshold, and the panel never re-computes that. A second opinion
about contrast would be a second answer to one question, and the day the two disagreed a site would
ship text nobody could read.

The refusal goes into a live region as well as beside the field, because a person who has just typed
a colour is looking at the field. A banner they are not looking at is a banner they will not read.
