# TokenSwatchGrid

A design system's tokens, as a grid a person can walk.

<Canvas of={TokenSwatchGridStories.ColourRoles} />

## Why a swatch is never a colour alone

A grid of coloured squares is unusable to anyone who cannot tell the squares apart — roughly one man
in twelve, every screen reader, and anybody on a bad monitor. So every cell carries the token's
**name** and its **value** as text, and the colour sits beside them as decoration.

That decision is also what lets measurements share the grid. `--t-gap` has no colour to show, and a
component built around swatches would have needed a second component for them.

<Canvas of={TokenSwatchGridStories.Measurements} />

## Why it is its own component

The panel that edits a design system is a form: a field, a commit, a refusal. This is a **navigation
surface**: a grid with its own keyboard model and its own focus. Folding the two together would have
produced one component with two reasons to change and two keyboard contracts, and the repository's
rule of one substantive component per file exists precisely to prevent that. It is a sibling of
`DesignSystemPanel` rather than a folder inside it, because the component contract discovers only
direct children of `components/` — a nested folder would never have had its five artifacts checked.

## The keyboard model

<Canvas of={TokenSwatchGridStories.Keyboard} />

Arrow keys move by row and column. `Home` and `End` go to the ends of the **row**, not of the grid.
Movement clamps at the edges rather than wrapping, because wrapping reads as the grid jumping.

Exactly one cell is tabbable at a time — a roving `tabindex` — so a person tabbing past sixty tokens
presses Tab once, not sixty times.
